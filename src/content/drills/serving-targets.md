A serve that lands in is worth more than a hard serve that sails out. This drill trains accuracy first.

## What you need

- A court and net
- As many balls as you can get
- Six targets: cones, towels or hoops

## Setup

Place a target in each of the six zones on the far side of the court: three near the net, three near the baseline.

The zones are numbered the way coaches call them. Zone 1 is back right (as seen from the receiving team), then counter-clockwise: 2 front right, 3 front middle, 4 front left, 5 back left, 6 back middle.

## How to do it

1. **Warm up (3 minutes).** Serve easy balls over the net with no target. Focus on a consistent toss.
2. **Deep zones (5 minutes).** Serve ten balls each at zones 1, 6 and 5. Count how many land within a step of the target.
3. **Short zones (4 minutes).** Serve at zones 2, 3 and 4. These need a softer touch.
4. **Call your serve (3 minutes).** Say the zone out loud before each serve, then try to hit it.

## Scoring

- 2 points: ball hits the target
- 1 point: ball lands in the right zone
- 0 points: anywhere else in the court
- Minus 1: net or out

Write down your score for 20 serves and try to beat it next time.

## What to watch for

- **The toss decides the serve.** Toss in front of your hitting shoulder, with no spin and the same height every time.
- **Step toward your target.** Your body should face where you want the ball to go.
- **Firm hand.** Contact the middle of the ball with a stiff wrist for a floating serve.

## Make it harder

- Serve after ten jumping jacks to practice under fatigue.
- Play a pressure game: you must hit three zones in a row before you may stop.
- With a partner, have them call the zone while your toss is in the air.

Beginners can serve from a few steps inside the baseline and move back as they improve.
