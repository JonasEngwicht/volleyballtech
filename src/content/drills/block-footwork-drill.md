Most missed blocks are footwork problems, not jumping problems. If you arrive late or still moving sideways, you jump into the net or leave a hole. This drill teaches the two blocking footwork patterns, the shuffle and the crossover, so they become automatic.

![Block footwork drill from above: three spots along the net, left, middle and right. The blocker starts in the middle, shuffles to one side and blocks, returns, then crosses over to the other side and blocks](/diagrams/block-footwork.svg)

## What you need

- A net (or a wall, with a line of tape at net height)
- Three markers: tape, cones or flat spots on the floor at left, middle and right front
- Court shoes with good grip

## Setup

Place the three markers about an arm's length from the net: one at the left antenna area (zone 4), one in the middle (zone 3) and one at the right (zone 2). Start in the middle in your blocking ready position: feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, hands up at shoulder height.

## How to do it

1. **Shuffle to one side.** Use quick step-close steps. Keep your shoulders square to the net and your hands up. Don't let your feet cross.
2. **Plant and jump.** Stop on the marker with both feet, then jump straight up and press your hands over the net.
3. **Land on both feet,** balanced, facing the net.
4. **Shuffle back to the middle.** Block again.
5. **Crossover to the other side.** For longer distances: open your near foot toward the side, cross over with your far foot, plant with the near foot and turn your hips back to the net before you jump.
6. **Block, land, return.** Alternate sides for 6 to 10 blocks, rest, then repeat.

## Progression

- **Level 1:** Footwork only, no jump. Walk through the steps.
- **Level 2:** Full speed footwork with a jump at each marker.
- **Level 3:** A partner or coach points left or right. React as fast as you can.
- **Level 4:** A coach on a box on the other side holds a ball over the net. Get there, jump and press your hands over the ball.
- **Level 5, middle blockers:** Add a quick attack approach after each landing. Middles block and then hit on almost every rally.

## What to watch for

- **Shoulders square to the net** when you jump. Turning sideways pushes the ball out of bounds.
- **Last step plants you.** Your final step stops your sideways movement, so you jump straight up.
- **Hands up the whole time.** Dropping your hands to run and raising them again costs time.
- **Arms near the net** before the jump, so you don't swing into it.

## Common mistakes

- Drifting sideways in the air and landing on a teammate or the center line.
- Crossing the feet during a short shuffle.
- Looking at the floor instead of the hitter.
- Landing on one foot. Blockers land on other players' feet more than anyone, which is how ankles get sprained. Practice the [safe landing drill](/drills/safe-landing/) too.

## Why it matters

A block that is a step late gives the hitter a free swing. Clean footwork lets a shorter blocker beat a taller one who moves slowly. Learn the full technique, hand position and blocking rules in [how to block in volleyball](/learn/how-to-block-in-volleyball/).

Many front-row players also wear ankle braces for this kind of work. See [how to choose volleyball ankle braces](/guides/how-to-choose-volleyball-ankle-braces/).
