Pepper is the warm-up you'll see at almost every volleyball practice and match. Two players keep one ball going with a dig, a set and a hit, over and over. It trains ball control, reading a hitter and quick reactions, and you can do it anywhere with one ball and a partner.

![Pepper drill from above: two partners 15 to 20 feet apart. A digs to B, B sets back to A, A hits a controlled ball at B. Then B digs, A sets, B hits](/diagrams/pepper.svg)

## What you need

- One volleyball
- A partner
- About 25 feet of open space (a gym, a backyard or a beach)

## Setup

Stand facing your partner, about **15 to 20 feet apart**. Beginners can start closer, about 10 feet, and move apart as control improves.

## How to do it

1. **B starts** with an easy toss or down ball to A.
2. **A digs** the ball to B with a forearm pass.
3. **B sets** the ball back to A, high and in front of A.
4. **A hits** a controlled down ball at B: standing, no jump, at about 60–70 percent power.
5. **Now swap roles.** B digs the hit to A, A sets it back to B, and B hits at A.

Every player does the same two jobs in turn: **dig, then hit** the set your partner gives back, and **set** the ball your partner digs to you. Keep the rally going as long as possible.

> Easy way to remember it: you dig it, your partner sets it back, you hit it.

## Progression

- **Level 1, catch pepper:** Dig, set, then the hitter catches the set and tosses a new ball to restart.
- **Level 2, standard pepper:** Dig, set, controlled hit, as described above.
- **Level 3, longer rallies:** Count your best streak and try to beat it every session. 20 contacts in a row is a good goal for beginners.
- **Level 4, harder hits:** Hit harder and at different spots, so your partner has to move.
- **Level 5, one-arm or overhead digs:** Allow overhead digs on hard hits, or add tips and roll shots.

## What to watch for

- **Dig to your partner, not over their head.** Aim your platform at their chest.
- **Set high and in front of your partner,** about 6 to 10 feet up, so they have time to hit.
- **Hit with control.** Pepper is about keeping the ball alive, not winning the point. Hit at your partner's middle, not their feet.
- **Talk.** Call "dig, set, hit" out loud at first to keep the rhythm.

## Common mistakes

- Standing too far apart too early. Control first, distance later.
- Hitting too hard, so the drill dies after two contacts.
- Sets that land behind the hitter, who then hits off balance.
- Flat feet. Stay on the balls of your feet so you can move.

## Why pepper works

Every contact in pepper is a real game skill: digging a hard ball, setting under pressure and hitting to a target. Because both players do all three, you get many touches in a few minutes. It's also a great test of your forearm passing; brush up with [how to pass a volleyball](/learn/how-to-pass-a-volleyball/) and [how to set a volleyball](/learn/how-to-set-a-volleyball/).
