Players practice jumping all the time and landing almost never. A few minutes of landing practice builds a habit that protects your knees and ankles for years.

This drill is general advice. If you have knee or ankle problems, check with a doctor or physical therapist first.

## What a good landing looks like

- **Two feet** touch down at nearly the same time
- **Balls of the feet first,** then the heels
- **Knees bent** and in line with your toes, not caving inward
- **Hips back,** chest up
- **Quiet.** A good landing is soft enough that you barely hear it

## How to do it

1. **Stick the landing (2 minutes).** Stand tall, drop quickly into a half squat and freeze for two seconds. Check your knees.
2. **Small jump and stick (2 minutes).** Jump a few inches, land softly and freeze. Do five, rest, repeat.
3. **Step off and stick (2 minutes).** Step off a low step or bench, land on two feet and freeze. Do not jump off.
4. **Block jump and stick (2 minutes).** Jump as if blocking, land and hold. Then add a sideways shuffle before the jump.
5. **Approach jump and stick (2 minutes).** Do your full approach, swing and land. Hold the landing for one second.

## What to watch for

- **Knees caving in.** Film yourself from the front. If your knees fall inward, slow down and go back a step.
- **Stiff legs.** Bend more. Your muscles should absorb the landing, not your joints.
- **One-foot landings.** They happen in games. In practice, make two feet the default.

## Make it harder

- Land and immediately shuffle to a new spot.
- Land, hold, then jump again.
- Add a quarter turn in the air and land balanced.

## When to do it

Add it to your warm-up two or three times a week. Do it while you are fresh, because quality matters more than quantity.
