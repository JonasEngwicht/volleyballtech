A good spike starts with your feet. This drill teaches the rhythm of the four-step approach without a ball, so you can focus on timing.

The steps below are for right-handed hitters. If you hit left-handed, mirror everything.

## What you need

- A few yards of open floor with good grip
- Court shoes
- Optional: tape to mark your start and take-off spots

## The footwork

1. **Right – small and slow.** A short, relaxed first step that gets you moving.
2. **Left – longer and faster.** This is your direction step. Aim it where you want to jump.
3. **Right – big and fast.** A long, low step. Swing both arms back behind you.
4. **Left – quick close.** Plant your left foot next to your right, slightly ahead and turned in, and jump straight up.

The rhythm speeds up: *slow … quicker … quick-quick*. The last two steps should almost land together.

## How to run the drill

1. **Walk it (2 minutes).** Go through the four steps slowly and say the rhythm out loud.
2. **Add the arms (3 minutes).** Arms swing back on step three and drive up on step four.
3. **Add the jump (5 minutes).** Do sets of five approaches at full speed. Rest briefly between sets.

## What to watch for

- **Jump up, not forward.** Land close to where you took off. Drifting forward takes you into the net.
- **Stay low on step three.** A long, low third step turns your forward speed into height.
- **Land on two feet.** Bend your knees and land softly on both feet at the same time.

## Make it harder

- Start from different spots to practice approaching from an angle.
- Have a partner toss a ball so you can match your approach to it.
- Film yourself from the side and check that your last two steps are quick and close together.

If landings hurt or you feel pain in your knees or ankles, stop and rest. Sharp or lasting pain is a reason to see a doctor or physical therapist.
