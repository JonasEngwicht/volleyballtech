Good defenders are not the ones who dive the most. They are the ones who get to the ball on their feet. This drill trains the low, quick shuffle that makes that possible.

## What you need

- About ten feet of floor
- Two markers (cones, shoes or tape)
- Court shoes with good grip

## Setup

Place the two markers about ten feet apart.

## The defensive posture

- Feet wider than your shoulders
- Knees bent, hips low
- Weight forward on the balls of your feet
- Arms loose in front of you, ready to form a platform

Stay this low for the whole drill. If your legs do not burn, you are standing too tall.

## How to do it

1. **Shuffle and touch (3 minutes).** Shuffle from one marker to the other and touch each one with your hand. Do not cross your feet. Work 20 seconds, rest 20 seconds.
2. **Shuffle and platform (3 minutes).** At each marker, stop, form your passing platform and hold it for a second.
3. **Forward and back (2 minutes).** Sprint three steps forward, then backpedal three steps, always low.
4. **React (2 minutes).** If you have a partner, have them point left or right. Without one, change direction every time you count to three.

## What to watch for

- **Do not bounce.** Your head should stay at the same height as you move.
- **Do not cross your feet** on short distances. Step with the lead foot, then follow.
- **Stop balanced.** Arrive with both feet set before you pass.

## Make it harder

- Have a partner toss a ball to each marker for you to pass.
- Add a sprawl or a controlled slide at one marker. Wear knee pads for this.
- Widen the markers to 15 feet and add a crossover step.

## Why it matters

Most passing errors start with the feet. If you arrive early and balanced, the pass is easy. If you reach, it is a gamble. Combine this drill with [wall passing](/drills/wall-passing/) for a complete solo passing session.
