## Who it is for

Serving is the one skill in volleyball that you control completely, and one of the few you can measure with a single number. The Ball Coach is a radar gun about the size of a phone. Point it along the path of the ball, and a second after contact it shows the speed.

That is useful for two kinds of people.

**Servers** who want feedback. Without a radar, "serve harder" is a feeling. With one, you can see that your toss a little further in front adds 3 mph, or that your hard serve and your controlled serve are closer in speed than you thought.

**Coaches** who run serving practice. A number on a display turns a routine drill into a challenge, and players compete with themselves. Pocket Radar quotes the long-time Penn State head coach Russ Rose, who used the Ball Coach to give players direct data on their serving mechanics.

The radar reads from 25 to 130 mph, and the maker gives an accuracy of plus or minus 1 mph. It can measure a ball flying toward it or away from it.

## Who should skip it

- **Beginners.** Until you can serve in nine times out of ten, speed is the wrong goal. Start with [how to serve a volleyball](/learn/how-to-serve-a-volleyball/).
- **Players who want jump data.** That is a different device: see the [VERT Jump Monitor](/gear/tech/vert-jump-monitor/).
- **Families on a budget.** At about $300, this is a tool for teams, clubs and very committed players.

## Setting it up for volleyball

A radar gun measures correctly only if the ball flies straight along its beam. Pocket Radar's volleyball guide gives these rules:

1. **Line it up.** Place the radar directly behind the server, or directly behind where the serve is aimed, in line with the ball's path.
2. **Match the height.** The radar should be at about the height where the hand meets the ball. In a gym, the maker suggests having someone sit high in the bleachers.
3. **Keep your distance.** Stay at least 15 to 20 feet from the ball. At 3 to 5 feet the beam is too narrow.
4. **Stay safe.** Put the radar where a ball cannot hit it or the person holding it.

The constant-on mode keeps the radar measuring without anyone pressing the button. On a tripod behind the end line, that lets one player work alone.

Then give the numbers a purpose. In our [serving targets drill](/drills/serving-targets/), write down the speed only for serves that land in the target zone. Fast serves that fly out do not count.

## How it compares

- **Against the Pocket Radar Smart Coach:** the Smart Coach connects to a phone app over Bluetooth, shows the speed in your video and can call it out loud. It costs more. Choose it if you want to record and share sessions.
- **Against the [VERT Jump Monitor](/gear/tech/vert-jump-monitor/):** different measurement. VERT counts and estimates jumps; the radar measures ball speed.
- **Against a tracking camera** such as the [XbotGo Chameleon](/gear/tech/xbotgo-chameleon/): video shows why a serve works; radar shows how fast it was. Coaches often want both.

## Good to know

The Ball Coach is best known in baseball and softball, and most listings show it with a pitcher. It works the same way for volleyball, tennis and other ball sports.

Prices vary more than usual. Stores that sell to schools list it for over $400, while consumer stores sell it for about $300, sometimes in a bundle with a tripod mount and case.
