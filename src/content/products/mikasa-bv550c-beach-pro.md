## Who it is for

International beach volleyball has one official ball, and this is it. The BV550C made its debut at the Beach Pro Tour Finals in Doha in January 2023 and went on to the World Championships and the Paris 2024 Olympics.

If you play in events that follow FIVB rules, training with the match ball is the main reason to buy it. A ball's weight, pressure and surface change how it floats on a serve and how it sits in your hands on a set. Practising with a different ball means adjusting again on match day.

Mikasa changed three things compared with the older VLS300:

- **Dimples.** The cover has the fine dimple texture that Mikasa already used on its indoor ball. It is the first time the company has put it on a beach ball. The aim is better grip for sets and soft shots.
- **Seams.** The stitching system keeps water out, so the ball does not gain weight in rain or after it lands in the sea.
- **Valve.** A new valve shape is meant to stop sand from getting in.

## Who should skip it

- **Casual players.** For the park, the backyard or a holiday, the [Wilson Soft Play](/gear/balls/wilson-soft-play-volleyball/) costs a fraction and is softer on beginners' arms.
- **Players on the American pro-style circuit.** The AVP uses the [Wilson AVP OPTX](/gear/balls/wilson-avp-optx/). Train with the ball your tournaments use.
- **Indoor players.** Beach balls are bigger and softer. For the gym you want an indoor ball such as the [Mikasa V200W](/gear/balls/mikasa-v200w/).

## Size, weight and pressure

The numbers are the official beach numbers: 66 to 68 cm around and 260 to 280 g, at a pressure of 0.17 to 0.22 bar. An indoor ball is slightly smaller and is pumped much harder.

Low pressure is what makes a beach ball feel soft and hang in the wind. Do not pump it up like an indoor ball. Use a pump with a gauge, and moisten the needle before you push it in so you do not damage the valve.

We explain the differences in detail in [indoor vs. beach volleyballs](/guides/indoor-vs-beach-volleyballs/) and the [volleyball size guide](/guides/volleyball-size-guide/).

## How it compares

- **Against the [Wilson AVP OPTX](/gear/balls/wilson-avp-optx/):** the two top beach balls. The Mikasa is the FIVB ball; the Wilson is the AVP ball and has graphics designed to make spin easier to see. Neither is "better". Pick the one your events use.
- **Against the Mikasa BV551C:** the replica looks the same and costs less. It is a reasonable choice for a club ball bag.

## Good to know

Mikasa says the cover is made with recycled nylon and that the printing uses solvent-free inks. After a session, rinse off salt water, brush off the sand and let the ball dry in the shade. Heat and direct sun age the cover faster than play does.
