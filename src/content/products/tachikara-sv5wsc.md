## Who it is for

In American high school gyms, Tachikara is one of the names you see most. The SV-5WS and its colored twin, the SV-5WSC, are the company's best-selling match balls.

The reason is the touch. Tachikara builds the ball with what it calls Loose Bladder Construction: the bladder is not glued to the cover but floats inside it. Together with the Sensi-Tec microfiber cover, that makes a ball that feels soft when it hits your arms. Younger players, who are still getting used to the sting of a hard-driven ball, notice the difference.

Buy it if:

- your school or league plays with Tachikara, and you want the same ball at home
- you want an approved match ball that costs less than the international models
- you want a ball in your team colors

## Who should skip it

- **Players in clubs or leagues that use Molten or Mikasa.** Practise with the ball you compete with. See the [Molten V5M5000](/gear/balls/molten-v5m5000/) and the [Mikasa V200W](/gear/balls/mikasa-v200w/).
- **Outdoor players.** Get a [Wilson Soft Play](/gear/balls/wilson-soft-play-volleyball/) for the backyard or a real beach ball for the sand.
- **Setters who want extra hand strength work.** That is a job for a weighted ball such as [Tachikara's The Setter](/gear/training-equipment/tachikara-the-setter-training-volleyball/).

## Size and weight

The SV-5WSC is an official size 5 ball: 25.6 to 26.4 inches around and 9.1 to 9.8 ounces. Those are the standard numbers for an adult indoor ball.

Players under about 12 often use a lighter ball. Our [volleyball size guide](/guides/volleyball-size-guide/) lists the right ball for each age group.

## How it compares

- **Against the [Molten V5M5000](/gear/balls/molten-v5m5000/):** the Molten is an FIVB-approved ball with a textured surface designed to steady its flight. It costs more. The Tachikara is softer and cheaper.
- **Against the [Mikasa V200W](/gear/balls/mikasa-v200w/):** the Mikasa is the international match ball, with 18 dimpled panels. It costs about twice as much. If you never play under FIVB rules, you do not need it.

## Good to know

Prices for this ball are confusing. The same model can cost $42 in one color and $60 in another, simply because some color combinations sell better. If you do not need team colors, choose the cheapest one.

Keep the ball indoors and do not sit on it. A composite cover that stays dry and clean keeps its grip for several seasons.
