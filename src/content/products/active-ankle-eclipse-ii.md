## Who it is for

Active Ankle makes the [T2](/gear/ankle-braces/active-ankle-t2/), the rigid brace most volleyball players know. The Eclipse II is its bigger sibling.

Where the T2 has two separate plastic sides, the Eclipse II has one solid U-shaped shell that runs under the foot. Hinges on both sides are set off-center to follow the ankle joint. Several straps, not just one, pull the brace against the leg. The maker says this gives the highest level of ligament support in its range while still letting the ankle move for jumps.

That makes it a brace for specific players:

- **Middle blockers and opposites**, who jump and land at the net more than anyone
- **Players whose ankles have rolled several times**
- **Athletes returning from a sprain**, when a professional recommends a rigid brace

A trade magazine for athletic trainers describes it as ideal for net players who need a higher level of support.

## Who should skip it

- **Players with healthy ankles who just want insurance.** The [Active Ankle T2](/gear/ankle-braces/active-ankle-t2/) or a lace-up brace is enough, costs less and goes on faster.
- **Back-row players.** Liberos rarely land in traffic at the net.
- **Anyone who dislikes straps.** One early reviewer admitted it took a while to work out how to put the brace on correctly.

## Sizing and fit

The open heel is the comfort feature here. Closed-heel braces can pinch at the back of the foot; the Eclipse II leaves that area free and uses a stirrup shaped to the foot instead.

The maker says the brace fits all shoe styles. In practice, any rigid brace needs space. Take your volleyball shoes with you when you try it on, or order from a store with easy returns. Low-top shoes with a normal fit are the safest match.

Put the brace on over a tall sock, set the straps from the bottom up, and walk around before you play. If something rubs in the first five minutes, it will rub more in the fifth set.

## How it compares

- **Against the [Active Ankle T2](/gear/ankle-braces/active-ankle-t2/):** more shell, more straps, more support, more money. Choose the Eclipse II only if you need the extra.
- **Against the [Zamst A2-DX](/gear/ankle-braces/zamst-a2-dx-ankle-brace/):** another strong-support brace. The Zamst comes in left and right versions and uses rigid side guards with X-straps rather than a hinge.
- **Against the [Ultra Zoom](/gear/ankle-braces/ultra-zoom-ankle-brace/):** the Ultra Zoom has a softer shell that molds to the ankle. Players who find hard plastic uncomfortable often prefer it.

Read [how to choose volleyball ankle braces](/guides/how-to-choose-volleyball-ankle-braces/) for the difference between lace-up, hinged and rigid designs.

## Good to know

The Eclipse line was developed with field trials on athletes, according to Cramer, the company behind the brand. The Eclipse I, with one hinge, was aimed at sports with more side-to-side movement. For volleyball, the maker points to the Eclipse II.
