## Who it is for

Every volleyball parent knows the choice: film the match, or watch it. The Chameleon is built to end that. You put your phone in the mount, set it on a tripod, choose the sport in the app and press record. The mount turns to follow the action, and you sit down.

Inside the unit is a motor and a separate AI processor. Your phone supplies the camera and the screen. Because the hardware is simple, the price is low for this kind of product: about $330 for the unit, against well over $1,000 for a dedicated tracking camera.

It makes sense for:

- **Parents** who want match video without holding a phone
- **Club and school teams** with no budget for a camera subscription
- **Players building a highlight video** for college recruiting
- **Coaches** who review matches during the week

## Who should skip it

- **Teams that want hands-off recording and analysis.** A dedicated camera such as the [Veo Cam 3](/gear/tech/veo-cam-3/) records the whole playing area and does not need anyone's phone, but it costs far more and needs a subscription.
- **People who need their phone during the match.** It will be on the tripod.
- **Coaches who want numbers, not pictures.** A stats app such as [SoloStats 123](/gear/tech/solostats-123/) is free and answers different questions.

## Setting it up for volleyball

Volleyball is a hard sport to film well. The court is short, players stand close together, and the ball travels up as much as it travels sideways. A few rules help with any camera:

1. **Go high.** Mount the camera behind one end line, as high as your tripod allows. XbotGo's bundle tripod extends from 4.5 to 13 feet. From a low position, the net and the near players block the far court.
2. **Check the frame before the match.** Both sidelines and the far end line should be visible when the camera is centered.
3. **Protect the tripod.** Put it where a shanked pass cannot reach it, and weigh the legs down if the gym allows.
4. **Bring power.** The mount runs about 8 hours. Your phone will not, so pack a power bank.

The tilt range is about 15 degrees up or down, so the height of the tripod decides how the court looks. Set that first.

## How it compares

- **Against the [Veo Cam 3](/gear/tech/veo-cam-3/):** the Veo records a wide view of the whole area and builds the follow-cam afterward, so nothing is missed. It is the choice for teams with a budget. The Chameleon follows the play live with a normal phone camera and costs a fraction.
- **Against a phone on a fixed tripod:** a fixed wide shot from behind the court is free and often good enough for volleyball, because the court is small. The Chameleon adds zoom and movement, and live streaming for relatives.
