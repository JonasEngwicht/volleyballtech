Your first volleyball shoes have one job: let you move and land safely on an indoor floor while you learn the game. You do not need carbon plates or the newest foam for that.

**How we chose:** These picks are based on manufacturer specs, retailer data and published reviews. We have not worn them ourselves.

## The short answer

- **Best first pair:** [ASICS Gel-Rocket 12](/gear/shoes/asics-gel-rocket-12/) – about $80
- **Cheapest sensible option:** [ASICS Upcourt 6](/gear/shoes/asics-upcourt-6/) – about $65, also in kids' sizes
- **If you already know you will play a lot:** [Mizuno Wave Momentum 3](/gear/shoes/mizuno-wave-momentum-3/) or [Nike HyperAce 3](/gear/shoes/nike-hyperace-3/)

## What a beginner needs in a volleyball shoe

1. **A non-marking indoor outsole.** This is non-negotiable. It grips the court and most gyms require it.
2. **A stable base.** You will be learning to shuffle, stop and land. A wide, flat-ish sole forgives clumsy footwork.
3. **A secure fit.** Your heel must not lift.
4. **Some cushioning.** Enough to take the edge off landings.

Everything else is a bonus at this stage.

## ASICS Gel-Rocket 12: the safe choice

The Gel-Rocket 12 gives you gel cushioning in the midsole and a midfoot support piece that keeps the shoe from twisting. It is a proper court shoe at a low price, and it comes in a wide version in men's sizes.

It is the pick for teens and adults who expect to play at least weekly.

## ASICS Upcourt 6: the budget choice

The Upcourt 6 is simpler: mesh upper, basic foam, rubber outsole. It is fine for PE, a first rec season or a child who may move on to another sport next year. The kids' version is called Upcourt 6 GS.

If you can afford the Gel-Rocket, we think the extra cushioning and support are worth about $15. See the full [comparison](/compare/asics-gel-rocket-12-vs-upcourt-6/).

## adidas Ligra 8: another budget option

The [adidas Ligra 8](/gear/shoes/adidas-ligra-8/) is adidas' answer to the Upcourt: a simple indoor court shoe for about $70, with a reinforced toe and a regular, forgiving fit. adidas also sells it in kids' sizes for about $60. Choose between the Ligra and the Upcourt by fit.

If you want a little more support without paying flagship prices, look at the [ASICS Gel-Tactic 12](/gear/shoes/asics-gel-tactic-12/) (list price $100, often discounted) or the [Mizuno Wave Dimension](/gear/shoes/mizuno-wave-dimension/).

## When it makes sense to spend more

If you have joined a club team that trains two or three times a week, buying a mid-range shoe right away can save you from replacing a starter shoe after a few months. The Mizuno Wave Momentum 3 and Nike HyperAce 3 are both reasonable step-ups.

## Mistakes to avoid when buying volleyball shoes

- **Buying running shoes.** They are built to roll forward and are unstable sideways.
- **Buying the pro model.** Specialist jump shoes reward technique you do not have yet.
- **Buying too big "to grow into".** A loose shoe causes blisters and rolled ankles. A thumb's width at the toe is enough.
- **Wearing them outside.** Dirt and asphalt ruin the grip quickly.

## For parents

Kids outgrow shoes faster than they wear them out, so a budget model is usually the right call. Check the fit every couple of months. If your child wears ankle braces, bring them when trying shoes on.
