Volleyball is played in **sets to 25 points, and you must win by 2**. A match is usually **best of five sets** (first to win three) or **best of three** (first to win two). If a match goes the distance, the deciding set is played to **15 points**, still win by 2. Every rally scores a point, no matter who served.

## How volleyball scoring works

Modern volleyball uses **rally scoring**:

- Every rally ends with a point for one team.
- The team that wins the rally serves next.
- If the receiving team wins the rally, it gets the point *and* the serve. That's called a **side-out**, and the team rotates one position.

Before 1999 (internationally) and the early 2000s (US college), volleyball used side-out scoring: you could only score when your team was serving, and sets went to 15. Rally scoring made matches shorter and easier to schedule for TV, and it is now used at every level.

## How many points is a set?

| Set | Points to win | Win by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regular set (indoor) | 25 | 2 |
| Deciding set (indoor) | 15 | 2 |
| Regular set (beach) | 21 | 2 |
| Deciding set (beach) | 15 | 2 |

There is no point cap in official play. If the score is 24–24, the set continues until one team leads by two: 26–24, 27–25, 33–31 and so on. Some recreational leagues and youth tournaments cap sets (for example at 27) to keep schedules on time.

## Best of three or best of five?

| Level | Usual match format |
| --- | --- |
| Professional and international | Best of five; fifth set to 15 |
| College (NCAA) | Best of five; fifth set to 15 |
| High school varsity | Mostly best of five; some states and JV teams play best of three |
| Club (youth tournaments) | Often best of three with the third set to 15, or two sets plus a tiebreaker |
| Adult recreational leagues | Varies: best of three, or a fixed number of sets per night |
| Beach | Best of three; third set to 15 |

Always check the tournament or league rules. Club tournaments in particular change formats to fit a busy schedule.

## How long is a volleyball game?

There's no game clock in volleyball, so match length depends on how close the sets are. Rough guide:

| Format | Typical length |
| --- | --- |
| One set to 25 | 20–30 minutes |
| Best of three (third to 15) | 45–75 minutes |
| Best of five (fifth to 15) | 1.5–2.5 hours |
| Beach, best of three | 40–60 minutes |

Long rallies, many timeouts, video challenges and close sets push matches toward the top of the range. A one-sided best-of-five match can be over in about 75 minutes.

## Switching sides

Indoor teams switch sides after each set. Before a deciding fifth set, the referee holds a new coin toss, and the teams switch sides when the leading team reaches 8 points.

In beach volleyball, teams switch sides every 7 points in sets one and two, and every 5 points in the deciding set, so that wind and sun affect both teams equally. See [beach volleyball rules](/learn/beach-volleyball-rules/).

## How points are won

You win a point when:

- The ball lands in the opponent's court (lines count as in).
- The opponent hits the ball out, into the net without it going over, or outside the antennas.
- The opponent uses more than three touches.
- The opponent commits a fault: net touch, center line violation, lift, double contact, overlap, or serving fault.

The most common ways to score are listed in the stats as:

- **Kill:** An attack that directly wins the point.
- **Ace:** A serve that wins the point directly.
- **Block:** A block that lands in the opponent's court.
- **Opponent error:** Everything else, from missed serves to net touches.

More terms like these are in our [volleyball terms glossary](/learn/volleyball-terms/).

## Common questions

**Is there a deuce in volleyball?** Not by that name. When it's 24–24, volleyball simply continues until a team leads by two.

**Can a set end 25–24?** No. With a one-point lead at 25–24, the set continues.

**What's the highest score in a set?** Long sets past 40 points have happened in pro volleyball, but they are rare.

**Who serves first?** A coin toss before the match decides which team serves first. In the following sets, the team that did not serve first in the previous set starts serving.
