Many volleyball shoes now come in two versions: a regular low-top and a mid-top with a higher collar. The mid usually costs a little more and weighs a little more. Is it worth it?

## What the collar actually does

A mid-top collar wraps around the ankle bones. That gives a more secure, locked-in feeling and can help hold your heel in place.

What it does not do is stop your ankle from rolling. A soft fabric collar is not strong enough to resist the force of landing on someone's foot. Players who want real protection against sprains use ankle braces, which are built for that job.

## Low-tops

**Pros**

- Lighter
- Full freedom of movement
- Easy to wear with ankle braces
- More models to choose from

**Cons**

- Less of a locked-in feel around the ankle

**Good for:** setters, liberos, players who wear braces, anyone who values speed.

## Mid-tops

**Pros**

- Secure, wrapped feel
- Can improve heel hold
- Some players simply feel more confident in them

**Cons**

- Heavier
- Can feel restrictive
- Often tight or awkward over ankle braces

**Good for:** middle blockers and hitters who like the feel and do not wear bulky braces.

## If you wear ankle braces

Choose low-tops in most cases. A brace needs room, and a mid-top collar fights with it. Bring your braces when you try shoes on, because you may need half a size more.

## If you have had ankle sprains

A shoe choice alone is not a plan. Talk to a doctor or physical therapist. They may recommend braces, and they will almost certainly recommend balance and strengthening exercises, which do the most to prevent repeat sprains.
