To block in volleyball, stand about an arm's length from the net with your hands up, move along the net to line up with the hitter's hitting arm, jump straight up just after the hitter jumps, and press your hands over the net, angled down and in, so the ball bounces back into the opponent's court. Then land on both feet.

Only front-row players may block. A good block doesn't always stop the ball; it also takes away part of the court and channels the attack to your defenders.

![Four phases of a volleyball block: ready position close to the net with hands at shoulder height, move with a side shuffle or crossover, jump with arms straight up, press the hands over the net and land on both feet](/diagrams/volleyball-block-steps.svg)

## Step 1: Ready position

- Face the net, about **an arm's length** away.
- Feet about shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent.
- Hands up in front of your shoulders, palms facing the net, fingers spread.
- Eyes forward: watch the pass, then the setter, then the hitter.

## Step 2: Read the play

Good blockers don't watch the ball the whole time. A simple reading pattern:

1. **Ball:** where does the pass go?
2. **Setter:** where does the set go? Front set, back set, quick set?
3. **Ball:** the path of the set.
4. **Hitter:** the hitter's approach angle and arm. This tells you where to block.

## Step 3: Move along the net

- **Side shuffle (step-close):** for short distances of one or two steps. Keep your shoulders square to the net.
- **Crossover step:** for longer distances, like a middle blocker moving to the outside. Step with the near foot, cross over with the far foot, then plant and turn square to the net.

Stop and plant **before** you jump. Drifting sideways in the air pushes you into the net and opens holes in the block.

## Step 4: Time the jump

As a rule of thumb, **jump just after the hitter jumps**. Against a fast quick set, jump with the hitter. Against a high, slow outside set, wait a little longer. Jump straight up with your hands already near the net.

Line up with the hitter's **hitting arm**, not their body. Against a right-handed hitter on the left side, your inside hand is roughly in front of the ball.

## Step 5: Penetrate and press

- Push your arms **over the net** into the opponent's space. You may reach over as long as you don't touch the ball before the attack.
- Hands firm, fingers spread and stiff, thumbs up.
- **Angle your hands down and in** toward the middle of the opponent's court, so the ball goes down instead of out.
- Keep your hands closer together than the width of a ball, so it can't pass between them.
- Shoulders up toward your ears to close the gap between arms and net.

## Step 6: Land and turn

Land on **both feet**, balanced. Then turn toward where the ball went and get ready to play it or to attack. Blockers get more ankle injuries than any other players, usually by landing on an opponent's foot under the net. ## Soft block vs. stuff block

- **Stuff block:** you reach over the net and send the ball straight down. That's the goal against a hitter you can reach.
- **Soft block:** if you're shorter or late, keep your hands up and tilted slightly back to deflect the ball upward so a teammate can play it.

## Blocking rules

- Only front-row players may block. The libero may never block.
- A block **doesn't count** as one of your team's three touches indoors. In beach volleyball, it does.
- The blocker may play the ball again right after the block.
- You may reach over the net only after the opponent has attacked, or used their three touches.
- **You can't block a serve.**
- Touching the net between the antennas while playing the ball is a fault.

## Common blocking mistakes

- **Swinging the arms** at the ball like a hit. Hold your hands firm and let the ball come to you.
- **Jumping too early.** You're on the way down when the hitter swings.
- **Reaching forward instead of up,** which leads to net touches.
- **Hands apart.** The ball goes straight through.
- **Watching only the ball.** You'll be late and in the wrong spot.
