Volleyball tech has grown quickly. A few years ago, measuring your jump meant chalk on a wall. Today you can count every jump in a practice, film a match without a camera operator and get statistics from a phone app.

This guide gives an overview of the main types of volleyball tech, what they do well and where their limits are. It describes categories rather than single products, because models and prices change fast.

## Volleyball tech at a glance

| Type | What it does | Most useful for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jump trackers | Estimate jump height and count jumps | Hitters, middles, coaches managing workload |
| Video analysis apps | Slow motion, drawing tools, side-by-side comparison | Every player working on technique |
| Auto-tracking cameras | Film matches and follow the play without an operator | Teams, parents, recruiting video |
| Stats apps | Record serves, passes, kills and errors | Coaches and team managers |
| Ball machines | Deliver repeatable serves, sets or attacks | Clubs and schools |
| Speed radars | Measure serve and spike speed | Servers, curious players |

## Jump trackers

A jump tracker is a small sensor worn on the waist, shoe or wrist. It estimates how high you jump and counts how often.

**What they are good for:** tracking jump load across a week, which is useful for avoiding overuse, and seeing trends over months.

**Limits:** The height is an estimate. Numbers from different devices are not comparable, so judge your progress against your own earlier results.

If you only want to know your vertical, a wall and chalk are free. See [how to jump higher for volleyball](/guides/how-to-jump-higher-for-volleyball/).

## Video analysis

For most players, video is the volleyball tech with the best value. Your phone already records slow motion.

- Film your approach from the side and your arm swing from behind.
- Compare your clip with a good example side by side.
- Use a cheap tripod so the angle stays the same every time.

Apps add drawing tools, frame-by-frame scrubbing and comparison views. Many basic versions are free.

## Auto-tracking cameras

These cameras or phone mounts follow the action on their own. They are popular with clubs and parents who want full-match video for review or recruiting.

**Things to check:** how well the tracking handles a volleyball court, how high the camera needs to be mounted, and whether a subscription is required for editing and sharing.

## Stats and scouting apps

Stats apps let a coach or parent tap in each contact and get a report afterward: serve percentage, passing average, hitting efficiency.

They are only as good as the person entering the data. Start with one or two numbers, such as serves in and passing quality, before tracking everything.

## Ball machines and training tech

Ball machines deliver the same ball again and again, which is ideal for passing and defense repetition. They are expensive and heavy, so they are mostly club and school equipment.

Simpler training gear often gives more value for individuals: a rebounder net, a setting target or just a wall. Try our [wall passing](/drills/wall-passing/) and [wall setting](/drills/wall-setting/) drills.

## Is volleyball tech worth it?

- **Beginners:** No. Spend the money on good [volleyball shoes](/gear/shoes/) and time on basics.
- **Club and school players:** Phone video, yes. A jump tracker is a nice extra if you like numbers.
- **Coaches:** Video and simple stats pay off quickly. Bigger purchases depend on your budget.

## How to choose

1. Decide which question you want answered, such as "How high do I jump?" or "Why are my passes off?"
2. Check whether your phone can already do it.
3. Look for ongoing subscription costs.
4. Read independent reviews, and be skeptical of accuracy claims.

Technology measures your game. It does not train it. The numbers only help if they change what you do in practice.
