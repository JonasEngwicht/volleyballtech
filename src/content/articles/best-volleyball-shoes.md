There is no single best volleyball shoe. A middle blocker and a libero need different things, and a beginner needs something else again. So instead of a ranking, this list sorts current shoes by who they suit.

**How we chose:** We compared current models from the four big brands using manufacturer specs, retailer data and independent reviews. We have not worn these shoes ourselves, and we say so on every product page. Each pick links to a page with full specs and a "who should skip it" section.

## Best for beginners: ASICS Gel-Rocket 12

The Gel-Rocket is the classic first volleyball shoe, and the twelfth version still does the job: indoor grip, some gel cushioning, a stable midfoot and a list price around $80. It also comes in a wide version, which is rare at this price.

**Skip it if** you already train several times a week and jump a lot. You will want more cushioning.

If money is very tight, the [Upcourt 6](/gear/shoes/asics-upcourt-6/) costs about $15 less. We explain the difference in [Gel-Rocket 12 vs. Upcourt 6](/compare/asics-gel-rocket-12-vs-upcourt-6/).

## Best all-rounder: Mizuno Wave Momentum 3

Most club and school players rotate through several positions. The Wave Momentum 3 handles all of them: foam cushioning for landings, a Wave plate in the heel for stability and a toe guard for players who drag their feet.

**Skip it if** you want the lightest shoe possible.

## Best for hitters: ASICS Sky Elite FF 4

ASICS relaunched its jump shoe in June 2026 with new FF BLAST MAX foam and a forefoot lacing system designed to keep the foot wrapped during take-off and landing. It comes as a low-top and as a mid-top (MT).

**Skip it if** you are a beginner. The older Sky Elite FF 3 is also still around at lower prices.

For very advanced hitters there is the [Metarise 2](/gear/shoes/asics-metarise-2/), with a stiff plate and curved toe. It is expensive and rewards a clean approach. See [Sky Elite FF 4 vs. Metarise 2](/compare/asics-sky-elite-ff-4-vs-metarise-2/).

## Best for setters and liberos: ASICS Netburner Ballistic FF 4

Back-row players and setters take hundreds of quick steps and comparatively few big jumps. The Netburner Ballistic is built for exactly that, with a flexible sole and a light upper.

**Skip it if** you prefer a flat shoe. It has a 15 mm heel drop.

## Best light flagship: adidas Crazyflight 7

The seventh Crazyflight is a full redesign with a full-length Lightstrike Pro midsole. The low version is among the lighter cushioned shoes on this list. The mid version trades some weight for a supportive collar.

**Skip it if** you want a firm, heavily structured shoe.

## Best from Nike: HyperSet 2 and HyperAce 3

The [HyperSet 2](/gear/shoes/nike-hyperset-2/) is the hitter's shoe: an Air Zoom unit under the forefoot and a strap that locks the midfoot down. Independent reviewers rate its grip and lockdown highly, but describe the fit as on the narrow side.

The [HyperAce 3](/gear/shoes/nike-hyperace-3/) is the lighter, cheaper all-rounder, sold in unisex sizing.

## How to choose the right volleyball shoes

1. **Start with how much you jump.** More jumping means you want more forefoot cushioning.
2. **Be honest about your level.** Flagship shoes do not make a beginner better.
3. **Fit beats features.** A cheaper shoe that fits is better than a flagship that does not.

Our guide to [choosing volleyball shoes](/guides/how-to-choose-volleyball-shoes/) explains grip, cushioning, support and fit in detail.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which brand makes the best volleyball shoes?

ASICS and Mizuno have the widest volleyball ranges. Nike and adidas offer fewer models, but their top shoes compete well. Fit differs more between brands than quality does.

### How much should I spend?

Around $65–$90 buys a solid first pair. Most regular players are well served between $110 and $150. Above that you are paying for the newest materials.
