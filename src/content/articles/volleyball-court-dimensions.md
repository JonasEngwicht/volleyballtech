An indoor volleyball court is **18 meters long and 9 meters wide (59 x 29.5 feet)**. A beach volleyball court is smaller: **16 x 8 meters (52.5 x 26.25 feet)**. The net splits the court into two squares, one for each team.

Below you'll find every line and zone explained, the free space you need around the court, and what to plan for if you are setting up a court in your backyard.

![Top view of an indoor volleyball court: 18 by 9 meters, net over the center line, attack lines 3 meters from the net on each side, free zone and service zones around it](/diagrams/volleyball-court-dimensions.svg)

## Volleyball court dimensions at a glance

| Measurement | Indoor (6v6) | Beach (2v2) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Court size | 18 x 9 m (59 ft x 29 ft 6 in) | 16 x 8 m (52 ft 6 in x 26 ft 3 in) |
| Each team's side | 9 x 9 m (29 ft 6 in square) | 8 x 8 m (26 ft 3 in square) |
| Attack line | 3 m (9 ft 10 in) from the net | None |
| Free zone (minimum) | 3 m (about 10 ft) on all sides | 3 m (about 10 ft) on all sides |
| Free zone at top international events | 5 m beside the sidelines, 6.5 m behind the end lines | 5–6 m |
| Line width | 5 cm (2 in) | 5–8 cm ribbon |
| Clear height above the court | At least 7 m (23 ft) | Open sky |

## Indoor court lines and zones

**Boundary lines.** Two sidelines and two end lines mark the 18 x 9 m playing court. The lines are part of the court: a ball that touches any part of a line is in.

**Center line.** The center line runs under the net and divides the court into two 9 x 9 m halves. Players may step onto the center line, and even past it, as long as part of the foot stays on or above the line. The [volleyball rules guide](/learn/volleyball-rules/) explains this fault.

**Attack line.** On each side, a line 3 meters (9 feet 10 inches) from the center line separates the front zone from the back zone. Back-row players must take off from behind this line if they want to attack a ball that is above the net. In international play, the attack line is extended outside the sidelines with short dashed lines.

**Front zone and back zone.** The area between the net and the attack line is the front zone. Everything behind it is the back zone. The front zone also extends into the free zone beyond the sidelines.

**Service zone.** The serve is taken from anywhere behind the end line, between the extensions of the two sidelines. It is 9 meters wide and runs back to the end of the free zone.

## How much space do you need around the court?

The **free zone** is the open space around the court where players can still play the ball. Rulebooks require at least 3 meters (about 10 feet) on every side. That makes the minimum total playing area **24 x 15 meters (about 79 x 49 feet)**.

Top international competitions use a wider free zone: 5 meters beside the sidelines and 6.5 meters behind the end lines. Many school gyms in the US have less space than this, especially behind the end lines. That's one reason you see servers standing right against the wall in a small gym.

Above the court you need at least 7 meters (23 feet) of clear height. Beams, lights and basketball hoops below that height interfere with high sets and serves.

## Net and antenna measurements

The net hangs directly above the center line.

- **Net width:** 1 meter (39 inches) top to bottom
- **Net length:** 9.5 to 10 meters (31 to 33 feet)
- **Side bands:** 5 cm wide white bands sit above each sideline
- **Antennas:** 1.8 meters long, sticking up 80 cm (32 inches) above the net, on the outside edge of each side band
- **Posts:** 0.5 to 1 meter outside the sidelines

The ball has to cross the net between the antennas. Net heights depend on gender and age; see our full table of [volleyball net heights](/learn/volleyball-net-height/).

## Beach volleyball court dimensions

Beach courts are **16 x 8 meters** with no attack line, because beach players don't have front-row and back-row restrictions. The boundary lines are ribbons staked into the sand, and the sand should be at least 40 cm (16 inches) deep at official venues.

The net height is the same as indoors: 2.43 m for men and 2.24 m for women. More on the differences in [beach volleyball rules](/learn/beach-volleyball-rules/).

## Are US courts different?

High school and college courts in the US follow the same 18 x 9 meter size. You'll sometimes see older gyms lined at 60 x 30 feet, a few inches longer and wider. For practice and recreational play, that difference doesn't matter.

## How big is a volleyball court compared to other courts?

A volleyball court is much smaller than a basketball court. A high school basketball court is 84 x 50 feet, an NBA or college court 94 x 50 feet. That's why many gyms fit two or three volleyball courts across one basketball floor.

| --- | --- |
| Indoor volleyball | 59 x 29.5 ft |
| Beach volleyball | 52.5 x 26.25 ft |
| Badminton (doubles) | 44 x 20 ft |
| Pickleball | 44 x 20 ft |
| Tennis (doubles) | 78 x 36 ft |
| Basketball (NBA / NCAA) | 94 x 50 ft |

## Setting up a backyard or grass court

You don't need a regulation court to have fun. For a backyard game:

1. **Pick the size.** For 6v6 on grass, aim for the full 18 x 9 m (59 x 29.5 ft). For 2v2 or 3v3, a beach-size court of 16 x 8 m (52.5 x 26.25 ft) plays better. In a small yard, shrink the court rather than crowding the edges.
2. **Leave room to run.** Try to keep about 2–3 meters (6–10 feet) free on each side, away from fences, trees and patios.
3. **Mark the lines.** Boundary kits with flat webbing and stakes are the easiest option. Spray paint for grass works too.
4. **Set the net height.** Use the men's height for men's and coed games, the women's height for women's games, and lower it for kids.
