Volleyball looks chaotic until you know who is supposed to do what. Each team has six players on the court, and at higher levels each one has a specialized job. This guide explains the positions in plain English.

## Volleyball positions at a glance

| Position | Main job | Key skills |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setter | Runs the offense, sets the ball to hitters | Quick feet, soft hands, decisions |
| Outside hitter | Main attacker on the left, also passes | Hitting, passing, all-round play |
| Opposite hitter | Attacks on the right, blocks the other team's outside | Hitting, blocking |
| Middle blocker | Blocks at the net, hits quick sets | Timing, side-to-side speed, jumping |
| Libero | Back-row defensive specialist | Passing, digging, reading the game |
| Defensive specialist | Back-row substitute for passing and defense | Passing, serving |

## Setter

The setter is the playmaker. They take the second touch and deliver the ball to an attacker. Setters cover a lot of ground, make constant small adjustments and need to stop precisely under the ball.

**Gear:** Light, flexible shoes with great grip. Slim knee pads.

## Outside hitter

Outside hitters attack from the left front and are usually the team's go-to scorers. They also pass serves and play defense, so they need the most complete skill set.

**Gear:** Cushioned shoes that handle a lot of approach jumps. Medium knee pads.

## Opposite hitter

The opposite plays on the right side, opposite the setter in the rotation. They attack, block the opponent's outside hitter and often hit from the back row.

**Gear:** Similar to outside hitters, with a focus on cushioning and stable landings.

## Middle blocker

Middles are the first line of defense at the net. They move sideways along the net to block and hit fast, low sets in the middle. They jump on almost every rally.

**Gear:** Stable, well-cushioned shoes. Many like a mid-top. Slim knee pads.

## Libero

The libero wears a different colored jersey and plays only in the back row. They can replace any back-row player without a formal substitution, and they are not allowed to attack the ball above the net. Their job is to pass and dig.

**Gear:** Low, fast shoes and thick, well-fitting knee pads.

## Defensive specialist

A defensive specialist is a regular substitute who comes in for the back-row rotations to pass and defend. Unlike the libero, they follow normal substitution rules.

## How rotation works

Each time your team wins the serve back, all six players rotate one spot clockwise. Three players are in the front row and three in the back. Only front-row players may block or attack the ball above the net from in front of the attack line.

Once the ball is served, players can move to their specialized spots. That is why you see everyone switch places right after the serve.

## Which position should a beginner play?

All of them. New players, and especially kids, learn the most by rotating through every spot. Specializing comes later, once you know what you enjoy and what you are good at.
