Buying a volleyball sounds simple until you see the options: indoor, beach, "lite", size 5, size 4. This guide explains what the numbers mean and which ball fits which player.

## Official volleyball sizes at a glance

| Ball | Circumference | Weight | Pressure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Indoor (adult, official) | 65–67 cm (25.6–26.4 in) | 260–280 g (9.2–9.9 oz) | 0.30–0.325 kg/cm² (4.3–4.6 psi) |
| Beach (official) | 66–68 cm (26–26.8 in) | 260–280 g (9.2–9.9 oz) | 0.175–0.225 kg/cm² (2.5–3.2 psi) |
| Youth "lite" ball | Same size as adult indoor | Lighter, roughly 7–8 oz | See the ball's label |

The indoor and beach figures follow the official FIVB rules. Youth ball weights vary by maker and league, so check your league's rules before you buy.

## Indoor vs. beach balls

They are not interchangeable.

- **Indoor balls** are smaller, harder and faster. Panels are usually glued to an inner lining, which gives a smooth, consistent surface.
- **Beach balls** are slightly bigger and much softer. They are stitched and water-resistant, and the lower pressure makes them float more in wind.

Using an indoor ball outside ruins it quickly. Sand, moisture and rough ground damage the cover.

## Which ball for which age?

- **Under about 12:** A lighter youth ball. It is the same size as a regular ball but easier on small arms, so kids can learn to pass and serve without pain.
- **12 and up:** A regular official-size indoor ball. In the United States, check which ball your club or school league uses.
- **Adults:** A regular official-size ball for indoor or beach.

Youth teams also play with a lower net. See our [volleyball net height chart](/learn/volleyball-net-height/) for every age group.

## Leather or synthetic?

- **Synthetic (composite) leather:** The standard today for most training and match balls. Durable and consistent.
- **Genuine leather:** Soft touch, used for some high-level match balls. More expensive and needs more care.
- **Rubber or foam:** Cheap and tough. Fine for the backyard and PE, not for serious practice.

## Training balls

- **Setter training balls** are heavier than normal to build hand and wrist strength.
- **Soft or oversized trainers** help young beginners lose their fear of the ball.

Heavier balls are for short, focused setting work. Do not serve or spike them.

## How to check the pressure

Too much air makes the ball sting. Too little makes it dead. A small pressure gauge costs a few dollars and takes the guesswork out. Wet the needle before you insert it so you do not damage the valve.

## Which volleyball should you buy?

- **For a child starting out:** One lighter youth ball.
- **For practice at home:** The same model your team plays with, so the feel matches.
- **For the beach or park:** A dedicated outdoor ball.
