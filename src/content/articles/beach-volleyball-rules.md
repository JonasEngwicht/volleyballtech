Beach volleyball looks like indoor volleyball on sand, but the rules change the game a lot. Two players cover the whole court, the block counts as a touch, and soft open-hand tips are not allowed. Here are the official beach volleyball rules, based on the international (FIVB) rulebook that most US tournaments also follow, and how they compare to indoor.

## Beach vs. indoor rules at a glance

| Rule | Beach | Indoor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Players per team | 2 | 6 |
| Substitutions | None | Allowed |
| Court size | 16 x 8 m (52.5 x 26.25 ft) | 18 x 9 m (59 x 29.5 ft) |
| Attack line | None | 3 m from the net |
| Sets | Best of three; sets to 21, third set to 15 | Best of five (or three); sets to 25, last set to 15 |
| Does the block count as a touch? | Yes | No |
| Open-hand tip (dink) | Not allowed | Allowed |
| Time to serve after the whistle | 5 seconds | 8 seconds |
| Switching sides | Every 7 points (every 5 in the third set) | After each set |
| Net height | Same: 2.43 m men, 2.24 m women | Same |
| Ball | Larger, softer, lower pressure | Smaller, firmer |

## Players and positions

A beach team has two players and no substitutes. If a player is injured and can't continue after the allowed recovery time, the team forfeits.

There are no fixed positions and no rotational overlaps. The only order that matters is the **serving order**: the two players must alternate serves as set at the start of each set.

## The court

The court is **16 x 8 meters** with no attack line, so either player may attack from anywhere. There is no center line under the net either. You may cross into the opponent's space under the net as long as you don't interfere with their play. Full measurements are in [volleyball court dimensions](/learn/volleyball-court-dimensions/).

## Scoring and sets

- Matches are **best of three sets**.
- Sets one and two go to **21 points**, win by 2.
- The third set goes to **15 points**, win by 2.
- Rally scoring: every rally wins a point.

Teams **switch sides every 7 points** in the first two sets (at 7, 14, 21, and so on) and every **5 points** in the third set. This keeps wind and sun fair for both teams. Each team gets one 30-second timeout per set. In international play there is also a technical timeout when the two scores add up to 21 in sets one and two.

## The block counts as a touch

This is the biggest tactical difference from indoor. In beach volleyball, **a block uses one of your team's three touches**. If your partner blocks the ball and it stays on your side, you have only two touches left. The blocker may make the next contact.

## No open-hand tips

You can't push the ball over the net with your open fingertips, the "dink" that is common indoors. Legal ways to place a soft shot include:

- **Knuckles** (a "knuckle shot" or "pokey")
- **Straight, stiff fingers** held together (the "cobra")
- The **heel or palm** of the hand in one clean contact
- A controlled **roll shot** with the full hand, contacted cleanly without catching

## Stricter setting rules

Referees judge hand sets on the beach more strictly than indoors. A set must be clean, without visible spin from a double contact. Many beach players bump-set their partner to avoid the risk.

If you **set the ball over the net** on purpose, the ball must travel in the direction your shoulders face (forward or straight back). Setting it sideways over the net is a fault, unless you were trying to set your partner.

## Hard-driven balls and double contacts

On a hard-driven attack, a defender may contact the ball with fingers or make a slight double contact while digging, as long as it's one action. On a regular ball, open-hand overhead receptions are judged like sets.

## Serving

- You have **5 seconds** after the whistle to serve.
- Your partner may not screen the server from the opponents' view. If the receivers ask, the partner must move.
- A serve that touches the net and goes over is in play.

## Equipment and clothing

Beach volleyballs are slightly larger and softer than indoor balls, with a lower air pressure, so they float more in the wind. Read [indoor vs. beach volleyballs](/guides/indoor-vs-beach-volleyballs/) for the details, or see our pick, the [Wilson AVP OPTX](/gear/balls/wilson-avp-optx/).

Players usually play barefoot. In hot sand, many wear sand socks, and sunglasses and hats are allowed.

## Recreational beach formats

Outside official tournaments, you'll also find 3v3, 4v4 and 6v6 games on sand. These often use a larger court, allow open-hand tips, and play sets to 21 or 25. If you're joining a local league, ask which rules apply before your first match.
