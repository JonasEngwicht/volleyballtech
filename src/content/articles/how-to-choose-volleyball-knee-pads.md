Knee pads are the second thing most players buy after shoes. They do not prevent knee injuries from jumping, but they do protect you from bruises and floor burns when you go down for a ball. That makes you far more willing to actually go for it.

## What knee pads do (and do not do)

**They do:** cushion the impact when your knee hits the floor and stop your skin from scraping.

**They do not:** support the joint. If you need help with a knee problem, that is a job for a doctor or physical therapist, and possibly a brace.

## The three decisions

### 1. How much padding?

- **Thick padding:** The most protection. Good for beginners who land hard on their knees and for liberos and defensive specialists who are on the floor all match.
- **Slim, low-profile padding:** Less bulk and more freedom to move. Popular with hitters and setters who rarely dive.

If you are new, start thicker. You will fall more often and less gracefully while you learn.

### 2. How long is the sleeve?

- **Short sleeves** are cooler and lighter.
- **Longer sleeves** stay in place better and cover more skin.

This is mostly personal preference. If your pads keep sliding down, try a longer sleeve or a smaller size.

### 3. What size?

Fit matters more than anything else. A pad that slides is useless.

1. Measure around your leg at the center of the kneecap, standing with your leg straight.
2. Compare with the maker's size chart. Sizes differ between brands.
3. If you are between sizes, most players prefer the smaller one, because pads stretch with use.

A good fit is snug without cutting off circulation. The padding should cover the kneecap and the area just below it, because that is where you land.

## Where exactly should they sit?

Many beginners wear their pads directly over the kneecap. Experienced players often wear them slightly lower, so the pad covers the bony area below the kneecap that hits the floor first in a slide. Try both and see what protects you.

## By position

| Position | Typical choice |
| --- | --- |
| Libero / defensive specialist | Thick padding, secure sleeve |
| Outside hitter | Medium padding |
| Setter | Slim to medium padding |
| Middle blocker | Slim padding |

## Care

- Wash them regularly. They hold sweat and start to smell quickly.
- Use cold water and let them air-dry. Heat breaks down the padding and elastic.
- Replace them when the padding has gone flat or the sleeve no longer holds.

## Good technique protects better than padding

Knee pads are a backup. Learning to move low and slide instead of dropping straight onto your knees protects you much more. Our [defensive shuffle drill](/drills/defensive-shuffle/) is a good place to start.
