To pass (or "bump") a volleyball, get your feet behind the ball, join your hands so your forearms form a flat platform, and let the ball hit your forearms just above the wrists while your legs push you toward the target. Your arms barely swing. The angle of your platform decides where the ball goes.

Passing is the first contact of almost every rally, so it's the most important skill in volleyball. Good news: it's also the easiest to practice alone.

![Four phases of a forearm pass: low ready position, move behind the ball and build the platform, contact on the forearms, finish by pushing with the legs toward the target](/diagrams/forearm-pass-steps.svg)

## Step 1: Get in a ready position

- Feet a bit wider than your shoulders, one foot slightly ahead (usually the right).
- Knees bent, weight on the balls of your feet, shoulders slightly in front of your knees.
- Arms relaxed in front of you, apart, ready to come together.

You should feel like you could move quickly in any direction. If your heels are down and your legs are straight, you'll be late.

## Step 2: Move your feet to the ball

Good passers move first and pass second. Shuffle or run so the ball comes to the middle of your body. **Reaching with your arms** to the side, instead of moving your feet, is the most common passing mistake.

Try to stop moving before the ball arrives, so your body is balanced at contact.

## Step 3: Build your platform

1. **Join your hands.** Place one hand in the other's palm with fingers overlapping, or wrap the fingers of one hand around a fist. Keep it simple.
2. **Thumbs side by side,** pointing down toward the floor and parallel. Pushing the thumbs down locks your elbows.
3. **Arms straight,** elbows locked, so your forearms form a flat surface.
4. **Shoulders forward.** Squeeze your shoulders slightly toward each other. This makes the platform wider and flatter.

## Step 4: Make contact

- The ball should hit your **forearms, about 2 to 6 inches above your wrists**, not your hands or your elbows.
- Contact the ball **in front of your body, below your waist**.
- **Angle your platform** toward the target. Think of your arms as a mirror: the ball bounces off at the angle you present.
- Watch the ball all the way onto your arms.

## Step 5: Push with your legs, not your arms

As the ball arrives, extend your legs slightly up and toward the target. Your arms move only a little and stay **below your shoulders**. A big arm swing sends the ball flying out of control.

Against a hard serve or spike, do the opposite: absorb the ball by keeping your arms still and letting your body give a little.

## Passing off the midline

When you can't get your body behind the ball, **drop the shoulder** closest to the target and tilt your platform so it still faces the target. The ball should still bounce off a flat platform pointing where you want it to go.

## Common passing mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swinging the arms | Pass flies too high or over the net | Lift with your legs, keep arms quiet |
| Bent elbows | Ball goes sideways | Thumbs down and together to lock the elbows |
| Ball hits the hands | Pass shoots off in a random direction | Contact higher up on the forearms |
| Standing straight | Late to the ball, low passes | Stay low with your knees bent |
| Reaching sideways | Pass goes off the court | Move your feet first |
| Hands apart at contact | Uneven platform, two contacts | Join your hands before the ball arrives |

## Bump vs. set: which one should you use?

Use a forearm pass for serves, hard-driven balls and anything below your chest. Use an overhead set when the ball is high, slow and you can get under it. Our guide on [how to set a volleyball](/learn/how-to-set-a-volleyball/) explains the overhead technique.

## Does passing hurt your arms?

At first, yes. Red forearms are normal for new players and get better within a few weeks as you learn to contact the ball in the right spot. If it keeps hurting, check that the ball isn't hitting your wrists. Many players also go to the floor when passing low balls, so a good pair of knee pads helps. See [how to choose volleyball knee pads](/guides/how-to-choose-volleyball-knee-pads/).

## Passing drills

- **[Wall passing](/drills/wall-passing/):** the best solo passing drill. All you need is a ball and a wall.
- **[Pepper](/drills/pepper-volleyball-drill/):** the classic partner drill for passing, setting and hitting control.
- **[Butterfly drill](/drills/butterfly-drill/):** a team drill for serve receive.
- **[Defensive shuffle](/drills/defensive-shuffle/):** builds the footwork that gets you to the ball first.

Practice 10 focused minutes a few times a week, and your passing will improve faster than any other skill.
