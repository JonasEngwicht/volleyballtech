For volleyball practice or tryouts, wear **indoor court shoes, knee pads, athletic shorts or spandex, a breathable t-shirt or tank top, and crew-length athletic socks**. Tie long hair back and take off all jewelry. That's it. You don't need team gear or expensive brands to look like a volleyball player on day one.

## The quick checklist

| Item | Must-have? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Indoor court shoes | Yes | Grip and lateral support on gym floors |
| Knee pads | Yes | Protect your knees when you go to the floor |
| Shorts or spandex | Yes | Freedom to move and squat |
| Breathable shirt or tank | Yes | Stays light when you sweat |
| Athletic socks | Yes | Prevent blisters, cushion landings |
| Sports bra | Yes, if applicable | Support for jumping |
| Hair ties | Yes, if you have long hair | Keeps hair out of your eyes |
| Ankle braces | If you've sprained an ankle before | Lower the risk of another sprain |
| Water bottle | Yes | Volleyball practice is longer and sweatier than it looks |

## Shoes: the one thing worth spending on

Volleyball shoes have gum rubber soles that grip polished gym floors and support quick side-to-side movement. Running shoes have tall, soft heels and slick soles that make ankle rolls more likely when you jump and land.

- **Beginners** can start with a court shoe that fits well, even an entry-level model. See [best volleyball shoes for beginners](/best/best-volleyball-shoes-for-beginners/).
- **Basketball shoes** are an acceptable backup. Read [volleyball shoes vs. basketball shoes](/guides/volleyball-shoes-vs-basketball-shoes/) for the trade-offs.
- **Keep them gym-only.** Wearing court shoes outside picks up dust and kills the grip. Our guide on [how to clean volleyball shoes](/guides/how-to-clean-volleyball-shoes/) helps when they get slippery.

Not sure where to start? Our [guide to choosing volleyball shoes](/guides/how-to-choose-volleyball-shoes/) explains fit, grip and cushioning.

## Knee pads

Every player goes to the floor sooner or later, and a hard gym floor is unforgiving. Even at tryouts, coaches expect you to dive for a ball. Bring knee pads, even inexpensive ones.

- Hitters and setters usually prefer slim, low-profile pads.
- Liberos and defensive players want more padding.

See [how to choose volleyball knee pads](/guides/how-to-choose-volleyball-knee-pads/) and our [best volleyball knee pads](/best/best-volleyball-knee-pads/).

## Shorts and spandex

**Spandex** (compression shorts) is the standard for girls' and women's indoor volleyball because it doesn't get in the way when you squat, dive or jump. Many players wear loose shorts over spandex at practice. **Athletic shorts** are perfectly fine for everyone, as long as they let you move freely and don't have zippers or big pockets.

Avoid long basketball shorts that hang below the knee: they catch on knee pads.

## Shirts

A lightweight, moisture-wicking t-shirt or tank top is ideal. Cotton works, but it gets heavy when it's soaked. Avoid baggy shirts that you can catch with your hands when passing or setting.

## Socks

Crew or mid-calf athletic socks are the most popular. They protect your skin from knee pads sliding down and from shoe rubbing. Thicker socks add a little cushioning for landings.

## Hair, jewelry and nails

- **Hair:** Tie long hair back. A headband helps with sweat.
- **Jewelry:** Take it all off: earrings, rings, necklaces, bracelets and watches. Referees won't let you play with it, and it's a real injury risk at the net.
- **Nails:** Keep them short if you can. Long nails break when setting.
- **Glasses:** Wear sports goggles or contacts if possible. Regular glasses can break.

## What to wear to volleyball tryouts

Tryouts are about showing coaches you can move and compete. Dress the same as for practice:

- Court shoes you've already broken in (not brand-new shoes on tryout day)
- Knee pads
- Spandex or athletic shorts, plain shirt
- Hair tied back, no jewelry

A few extra tips:

- **Bright or plain colors.** Some coaches assign numbers; wearing something simple makes the pinned number easy to read.
- **Bring water and a snack.** Tryouts can run two hours or more.
- **Bring a backup:** an extra hair tie, socks and a shirt.
- **Arrive early** and warm up. Show you know the basics: review [volleyball positions](/guides/volleyball-positions-explained/), the [rotation](/learn/volleyball-rotation/) and the [basic rules](/learn/volleyball-rules/) the night before.

## What to wear for game day

On game day, your team will usually wear matching jerseys and spandex or shorts. The libero wears a jersey in a contrasting color. Everything else stays the same: court shoes, knee pads, socks, no jewelry.

## Beach volleyball: what to wear

Beach players usually play barefoot in athletic swimwear, shorts and a tank top. In hot sand, sand socks protect your feet. Add sunglasses, a visor or hat, and plenty of sunscreen. More in our [beach volleyball rules](/learn/beach-volleyball-rules/).

## What to pack in your volleyball bag

- Court shoes (carried in, not worn outside)
- Knee pads and an extra pair of socks
- Ankle braces, if you use them
- Water bottle
- Hair ties, a towel, deodorant
- A snack for long practices
- Athletic tape and a few bandages
