To spike a volleyball, take a fast approach toward the net (left-right-left for a right-handed hitter, with the last two steps quick), jump off both feet, swing both arms up, draw your hitting elbow back high, and hit the top back of the ball at the highest point you can reach, snapping your wrist so the ball goes down into the court.

A good spike, or attack, is mostly about timing and footwork. Height and power come later.

![Four phases of a volleyball spike: approach steps, a quick right-left plant with both arms swinging back, two-foot jump with arms up and hitting elbow drawn back, contact at the highest point with a wrist snap](/diagrams/spike-approach-steps.svg)

The steps below are for right-handed hitters. If you hit left-handed, mirror everything.

## Step 1: Start from the right spot

Outside hitters usually start their approach **about 10–15 feet from the net**, outside the court on the left side. Wait there until the ball has been passed and you can see where the set is going. Starting too early is the most common timing mistake.

## Step 2: The approach

A four-step approach for right-handed hitters goes **right, left, right-left**. Beginners often use a three-step version: **left, right-left**.

1. **First steps (right, left):** small and slow. They set the direction toward where the set will come down.
2. **Long step (right):** the longest and fastest step. Swing both arms back behind you as you take it.
3. **Close (left):** bring your left foot down quickly next to and slightly ahead of the right. This "right-left" should be a quick one-two.

Your speed builds from slow to fast. Think "slow, slow, quick-quick". Our [approach footwork drill](/drills/approach-footwork/) lets you practice this without a ball.

## Step 3: Plant and jump

On the last two steps, plant your heels first and roll onto your toes. Your knees bend, your hips drop, and your arms swing back as far as they comfortably go. Then:

- Swing both arms forward and up hard.
- Explode straight up off both feet.
- Jump **up**, not forward into the net. If you keep drifting into the net, plant your feet slightly further back.

## Step 4: The arm swing

As you rise:

1. Both arms go up in front of you.
2. Your left arm points up at the ball to help you aim and balance.
3. Pull your right elbow **back and high**, above your shoulder, with your hand near your ear. This is the "bow and arrow" position.
4. Rotate your shoulders and torso back slightly.

## Step 5: Contact

- Swing your hitting arm forward fast, leading with the elbow, while you pull the left arm down.
- Hit the ball **at full reach**, in front of your hitting shoulder.
- Contact the **top back** of the ball with an open, firm hand. Your whole palm and fingers should be on the ball.
- **Snap your wrist** over the ball to add topspin, so it drops into the court.

## Step 6: Land safely

Land on **both feet**, knees bent, absorbing the impact. Landing on one foot, especially under the net, is how most ankle sprains happen. Practice it with our [safe landing drill](/drills/safe-landing/), and consider an ankle brace if you've sprained an ankle before; see [how to choose volleyball ankle braces](/guides/how-to-choose-volleyball-ankle-braces/).

## Common hitting mistakes

| Mistake | Result | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting the approach too early | Running under the ball | Wait until the set leaves the setter's hands |
| Slow last two steps | Low jump | Make the right-left plant quick |
| Jumping forward | Net touches, hitting from behind your head | Plant further back, jump straight up |
| Dropping the elbow | Weak hits that go into the net | Elbow high and back before swinging |
| Ball behind your head | Hits go long | Time the approach so the ball is in front |
| No wrist snap | Hits sail out | Snap over the top of the ball |

## Shot options beyond the hard spike

Once you can hit cleanly, add variety:

- **Line shot:** down the sideline, past the outside blocker.
- **Cross-court:** diagonally across the court, the most common angle.
- **Tip:** a soft open-hand push over the block. (Not allowed in beach volleyball.)
- **Roll shot:** a soft, high topspin shot into open space.
- **Tool:** hit off the blocker's hands so the ball flies out of bounds. That's your point.

## How to jump higher for hitting

Approach technique adds more height than anything else at first. After that, strength and power training help. ## Solo hitting drills

- **[Wall spiking](/drills/wall-spiking-drill/):** groove your arm swing and wrist snap against a wall.
- **[Approach footwork](/drills/approach-footwork/):** build a fast, consistent approach.
- **Toss and hit:** toss to yourself and hit from a standing position into the court to practice contact.
