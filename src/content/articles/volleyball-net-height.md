The official volleyball net height is **7 feet 11⅝ inches (2.43 m) for men** and **7 feet 4⅛ inches (2.24 m) for women**. Beach volleyball uses the same heights. Youth players use lower nets, usually starting around 7 feet for 12-and-under teams.

## Volleyball net height chart

| Level | Net height (feet) | Net height (meters) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Men (adult, college, pro) | 7 ft 11⅝ in | 2.43 m |
| Women (adult, college, pro) | 7 ft 4⅛ in | 2.24 m |
| High school boys | 7 ft 11⅝ in | 2.43 m |
| High school girls | 7 ft 4⅛ in | 2.24 m |
| Coed (adult) | 7 ft 11⅝ in (men's height) | 2.43 m |
| Reverse coed | 7 ft 4⅛ in (women's height) | 2.24 m |
| Beach, men | 7 ft 11⅝ in | 2.43 m |
| Beach, women | 7 ft 4⅛ in | 2.24 m |
| Sitting volleyball, men | about 3 ft 9 in | 1.15 m |
| Sitting volleyball, women | about 3 ft 5 in | 1.05 m |

## Net height for kids and youth teams

Youth heights are set by each league or club region, so they vary a little. These are the heights most US club and school programs use:

| Age group | Typical net height | Meters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 and under | 6 ft 6 in | 1.98 m |
| 11 and 12 | 7 ft 0 in | 2.13 m |
| 13 and 14 (girls and boys) | 7 ft 4⅛ in | 2.24 m |
| 15 and up, girls | 7 ft 4⅛ in | 2.24 m |
| 15 and up, boys | Moves up to 7 ft 11⅝ in, depending on region | 2.43 m |
| Middle school | Usually 7 ft 0 in to 7 ft 4⅛ in | 2.13–2.24 m |

**Check before your season.** Your league's rulebook is the final word. If you coach young beginners and have no rule to follow, lower the net until most kids can get a serve over it. Success early on keeps them coming back.

Younger players should also use a lighter ball. Our [volleyball size guide](/guides/volleyball-size-guide/) explains which ball fits which age.

## Why are men's and women's nets different?

The difference, about 19 cm or 7.5 inches, reflects average differences in height and jumping reach. It keeps the balance between attackers and blockers similar in both games. Without it, women's volleyball would have far fewer kills from front-row attackers.

## How to measure volleyball net height

Net height is measured in the **middle of the court**, from the floor to the top of the net.

1. **Tension the net first.** A loose net sags in the middle and gives you a false reading.
2. **Measure at the center.** Hold a tape measure or a net measuring stick straight up from the floor to the top edge of the net, right above the center line.
3. **Check both ends.** Measure again above each sideline. The ends may sit slightly higher, but by no more than 2 cm (about ¾ inch) above the official height.
4. **Adjust and re-check.** Raise or lower the net on both posts evenly, then measure the middle again.

On grass or sand, measure from the playing surface where players stand, not from a hole or bump.

## Net height and the rest of the court

The net is 1 meter (39 inches) wide from top to bottom and sits over the center line. Two antennas mark the sides of the crossing space: the ball must pass between them. You'll find the full layout in our [volleyball court dimensions guide](/learn/volleyball-court-dimensions/).

## Training at home with a lower net

If you practice in the backyard, you don't need a regulation net for every drill. Passing, setting and serving accuracy work fine with a lower net. When you work on hitting, raise it to your game height so your approach and timing transfer to the court.
