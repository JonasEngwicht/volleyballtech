Let's be honest first: no shoe will make you jump much higher. Your vertical comes from strength, technique and practice. What a good jump shoe does is help you plant hard without sliding, return a little energy and soften hundreds of landings.

**How we chose:** These picks are based on manufacturer specs, retailer data and published reviews. We have not worn them ourselves.

## What matters in a volleyball shoe for jumping

- **Forefoot cushioning.** You take off and land on the front of your foot.
- **Lockdown.** Your foot must not slide inside the shoe when you plant.
- **Grip.** A slipping plant foot costs height and risks injury.
- **A stable landing base.** You will land off balance sooner or later.

## ASICS Sky Elite FF 4

The default pick for most front-row players. ASICS designed it around the jump: springy foam, a midfoot structure meant to turn forward speed into height and a lacing system that wraps the forefoot. Middles who want more ankle coverage can choose the MT version.

## ASICS Metarise 2

The specialist. A stiff plate and a curved toe help experienced hitters roll into take-off. It is expensive, and it suits players with a fast, consistent approach. Everyone else gets more from the Sky Elite. We compare both in [Sky Elite FF 4 vs. Metarise 2](/compare/asics-sky-elite-ff-4-vs-metarise-2/).

## Nike HyperSet 2

The Air Zoom unit under the forefoot gives a springy feel, and reviewers single out the lockdown and indoor traction. The fit is on the narrow side.

## adidas Crazyflight 7

Lighter than most cushioned flagships in its low version. Choose the mid if you want a supportive collar for landings at the net.

## Mizuno Wave Momentum 3

Less of a pure jump shoe, more of a cushioned all-rounder. A good choice if you hit in the front row and pass in the back.
