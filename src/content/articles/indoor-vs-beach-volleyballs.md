An indoor volleyball and a beach volleyball look alike, but they play differently. This guide explains how they differ and which one you need.

## Indoor vs. beach volleyball at a glance

| | Indoor | Beach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Circumference | 65–67 cm | 66–68 cm |
| Weight | 260–280 g | 260–280 g |
| Pressure | 0.30–0.325 kg/cm² | 0.175–0.225 kg/cm² |
| Feel | Harder, faster | Softer, floats more |
| Panels | Usually glued | Usually sewn |
| Where to play | Gym, hard court | Sand, grass, outdoors |

These are the official FIVB figures. See our [volleyball size guide](/guides/volleyball-size-guide/) for more detail.

## Size and pressure

The beach ball is slightly bigger and much softer. The lower pressure slows it down and helps it hold up in wind.

## Construction

Indoor balls typically have glued panels for a smooth surface. Beach balls are sewn and made to handle sand and moisture.

## Can you swap them?

Not well. An indoor ball used on sand wears out quickly. A beach ball indoors feels soft and slow.

- **For the gym or club:** An indoor ball. See the [Mikasa V200W](/gear/balls/mikasa-v200w/) and [Molten V5M5000](/gear/balls/molten-v5m5000/).
