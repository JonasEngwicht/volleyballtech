Choosing the best volleyball depends on where you play. Here are three balls the pros and clubs use, and who should buy each.

**How we chose:** These picks are based on manufacturer specs, retailer data and published information. We have not played with them ourselves.

## The short answer

- **Best indoor ball:** [Mikasa V200W](/gear/balls/mikasa-v200w/): the Tokyo 2020 Olympic match ball
- **Best alternative indoor ball:** [Molten V5M5000](/gear/balls/molten-v5m5000/): FIVB approved with FLISTATEC design
- **Best beach ball:** [Wilson AVP OPTX](/gear/balls/wilson-avp-optx/): the official AVP tour ball

## Mikasa V200W

An 18-panel microfiber ball with a dimpled surface. If your club uses Mikasa, practice with this one.

## Molten V5M5000

A competition ball with a hexagonal surface pattern meant to steady its flight. It is a good choice if your league uses Molten.

## Wilson AVP OPTX

A sewn beach ball with high-contrast graphics that make spin easy to read. It is designed for sand, not for gyms.

## More volleyballs worth a look

- **[Mikasa BV550C Beach Pro](/gear/balls/mikasa-bv550c-beach-pro/):** the official FIVB beach ball, used at the Paris 2024 Olympics.
- **[Tachikara SV-5WSC](/gear/balls/tachikara-sv5wsc/):** a soft, NFHS-approved indoor ball in team colors, popular with high schools.
- **[Wilson Soft Play](/gear/balls/wilson-soft-play-volleyball/):** a cheap, soft recreational ball in official size, for the backyard and the beach.

## Do you need a pro ball?

Not necessarily. Beginners and casual players do fine with cheaper balls. A match ball makes sense when you want the same feel as your league.
