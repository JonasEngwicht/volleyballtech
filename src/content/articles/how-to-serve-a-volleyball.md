To serve a volleyball, stand behind the end line, toss the ball (or hold it, for an underhand serve) and hit it with a firm hand so it clears the net and lands in the opponent's court. Beginners start with the **underhand serve**, then move on to the **overhand float serve**, which is the most common serve at every level.

Below are step-by-step instructions for each serve, written for right-handed players. Left-handed? Mirror everything.

## The four main volleyball serves

| Serve | How it flies | Difficulty | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Underhand | High, slow arc | Easy | First-time players, young kids |
| Overhand float | Flat, no spin, wobbles unpredictably | Medium | Almost everyone from about age 11 up |
| Topspin | Fast, dips down sharply | Hard | Strong servers who want power |
| Jump serve (float or topspin) | Like the standing version, but faster and from higher | Hardest | Experienced high school, college and adult players |

## How to serve underhand

The underhand serve is the easiest way to get the ball over the net. It's slow and easy to pass, so you'll outgrow it, but it's the right first step.

1. **Stand facing the net** behind the end line, with your left foot slightly in front.
2. **Hold the ball in your left hand** at waist height, in front of your right hip. Keep that arm still.
3. **Make a fist** with your right hand, thumb on top of your bent fingers, or use the heel of a flat hand.
4. **Swing your right arm back**, then forward like a pendulum, keeping it straight.
5. **Hit the ball out of your hand** just below its center. Don't toss it.
6. **Step forward** with your left foot as you swing and follow through toward your target.

**Tip:** If the ball goes too high, hit a little closer to its center. If it hits the net, hit slightly lower on the ball and swing through.

## How to do an overhand float serve

The float serve hits the ball without spin, so it moves unpredictably in the air, a bit like a knuckleball. It's accurate, hard to pass and the serve most players use.

![Four phases of an overhand float serve: stance with left foot forward, low toss in front of the hitting shoulder, elbow high and arm drawn back, firm flat-hand contact](/diagrams/overhand-serve-steps.svg)

1. **Stance.** Left foot in front, pointing at your target. Hold the ball in your left hand in front of your right (hitting) shoulder.
2. **Toss.** Toss the ball low and straight, about an arm's length above your head and a little in front of your hitting shoulder. Most bad serves start with a bad toss.
3. **Draw back.** Lift your hitting arm with the elbow high, at least at ear level, and your hand open and firm. It looks like drawing a bow.
4. **Step and hit.** Step onto your left foot and swing forward. Hit the **center of the ball** with the heel of a firm, flat hand.
5. **Stop the hand.** Keep your wrist stiff and use a short follow-through. A big follow-through or wrist snap puts spin on the ball, and spin stops the float.

**Tip:** The float comes from a clean, centered hit. If the ball spins, your hand was off-center or your wrist was loose.

## How to hit a topspin serve

The topspin serve spins forward and dives down late. It's faster than a float but less forgiving.

1. Use the same stance as the float serve.
2. **Toss higher** and slightly further in front, so you can reach up and over the ball.
3. **Hit the lower back of the ball** with an open hand, then roll your hand over the top.
4. **Snap your wrist** forward and follow through down toward the floor.

Most players learn topspin only after their float serve is consistent.

## How to do a jump serve

A jump serve combines a serve with a spike approach. The **jump float** uses a short two- or three-step approach and a one-foot or two-foot takeoff; the **jump topspin** uses a full spike approach and a high toss.

1. **Toss first,** then approach. The toss sets the rhythm, so practice it alone until it lands in the same spot every time.
2. **Take off behind the end line.** You may land inside the court; only the takeoff must be behind the line.
3. **Hit at the top of your jump** with the same contact as the standing version: flat and centered for a float, wrist snap for topspin.

If your [approach footwork](/drills/approach-footwork/) isn't solid yet, learn that first.

## Serving rules to know

- Serve from behind the end line, anywhere across the width of the court.
- You have **8 seconds** after the referee's whistle (5 seconds in beach volleyball).
- Don't step on or over the line before contact.
- A serve that touches the net and goes over is **in play**.
- The serve must cross between the antennas.

## Common serving mistakes

- **Inconsistent toss.** If your toss moves, your contact moves. Practice tossing and letting the ball drop: it should land in front of your front foot every time.
- **Dropping the elbow.** A low elbow makes you push the ball instead of hitting it.
- **Swinging too hard.** Serving in is worth more than serving hard. A missed serve is a free point for the other team.
- **Looking at the target, not the ball.** Watch the ball until you hit it.
- **Rushing.** Use your 8 seconds. Breathe, pick a target, then serve.

## How to practice serving

- **Serve to targets.** Our [serving targets drill](/drills/serving-targets/) turns practice into a game.
- **Serve short of the net first.** Start halfway between the net and the end line, then move back as you get consistent.
- **Use a wall at home.** Serve against a wall from about 20 feet to groove your toss and contact.
- **Count your percentage.** Aim for 9 of 10 serves in before you add power.
