Volleyball is a cheap sport to start. You need very little gear for your first practice, and most of the rest can wait until you know you will stick with it. This checklist sorts volleyball gear into three groups: buy now, buy later and skip.

## Essential volleyball gear

| Gear | Why you need it | Rough cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Volleyball shoes | Grip and safe landings on indoor courts | $65–$150 |
| Knee pads | Protect against bruises and floor burns | $15–$40 |
| Athletic clothes | Shorts or leggings and a shirt you can move in | Whatever you own |
| Water bottle | Practices are long and gyms are warm | A few dollars |

### Volleyball shoes

Shoes are the one piece of volleyball gear worth spending real money on. A proper court shoe has a soft, non-marking rubber sole for grip and cushioning under the forefoot for landings. Running shoes are not a substitute.

Start with our [beginner volleyball shoe picks](/best/best-volleyball-shoes-for-beginners/) or read [how to choose volleyball shoes](/guides/how-to-choose-volleyball-shoes/).

### Knee pads

Beginners fall a lot, and knee pads make that painless. Fit matters more than brand. See [how to choose volleyball knee pads](/guides/how-to-choose-volleyball-knee-pads/).

## Volleyball gear to buy later

- **Your own ball.** Great for practicing at home. Get the same type your team uses. Our [volleyball size guide](/guides/volleyball-size-guide/) explains the options.
- **A gear bag.** Any backpack works at first. A separate shoe compartment keeps your court shoes clean.
- **Ankle braces.** Common at higher levels and for players with a history of sprains. Ask a doctor or physical therapist if you are unsure.
- **Volleyball socks.** Cushioned crew socks help prevent blisters.
- **Arm sleeves.** Some players like them for passing and for sliding on the floor. Purely optional.

## Volleyball tech and training gear

Once you play regularly, a few tools can make practice more effective:

- A phone tripod for filming your technique
- A ball pump and pressure gauge
- A jump tracker or video app to measure progress

None of this is needed at the start. Our [volleyball tech guide](/guides/volleyball-tech-guide/) explains what each gadget does and who it is for.

## Gear you can skip

- **Expensive flagship shoes** in your first season
- **Weighted training balls** unless a coach asks for them
- **Jump-training shoes and ankle weights**
- **Team-branded extras** you are not required to buy

## Beach volleyball gear

Beach volleyball needs even less: a beach ball, sunglasses, sunscreen and, on hot or cold sand, sand socks. You play barefoot, so no shoes are required.

## Checklist for your first practice

1. Indoor court shoes with non-marking soles
2. Knee pads
3. Shorts or leggings and a T-shirt
4. Water bottle
5. Hair ties if you need them
6. A small towel

Not sure what to put on? Read [what to wear for volleyball practice and tryouts](/guides/what-to-wear-for-volleyball/). Ask your coach or league before buying anything team-specific. Many clubs provide jerseys and balls.
