Short answer: yes, you can play volleyball in basketball shoes, and plenty of players at every level do. Both sports are played on indoor courts and involve jumping and quick cuts. But the shoes are not the same, and the differences matter more for some players than others.

## Volleyball shoes vs. basketball shoes: the main differences

| | Volleyball shoes | Basketball shoes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weight | Usually lighter | Usually heavier |
| Cushioning | Focused on the forefoot | Often thick through the whole sole |
| Height | Mostly low-top, some mid-top | Low, mid and high-top |
| Outsole | Soft rubber tuned for clean indoor floors | Often harder rubber, some made for outdoor courts |
| Feel | Low and close to the floor | Higher off the floor |
| Choice | Small range | Huge range of models and colors |

## Why some players prefer basketball shoes

- **More cushioning.** Heavy hitters and players with sore knees often like the thicker soles.
- **More choice.** There are far more models, widths and colors.
- **Availability.** Every sports shop stocks them. Volleyball shoes often have to be ordered.

## Why volleyball shoes are still worth it

- **They are lighter.** Over a long match or tournament day, that adds up.
- **They sit lower.** A lower sole feels more stable when you shuffle and land.
- **They are built for volleyball movement.** Short sprints, lots of side steps and straight-up jumps rather than long runs up the court.
- **The grip is tuned for indoor floors.** Some basketball outsoles are made for outdoor concrete and feel slick in a gym.

## By position

- **Liberos and setters:** A light, low volleyball shoe is usually the better choice. Quick first steps matter more than cushioning.
- **Outside hitters and opposites:** Either works. Pick by fit and how much cushioning you like.
- **Middle blockers:** Many like the extra cushioning and collar height of a basketball shoe. A mid-top volleyball shoe does a similar job at less weight.

## If you go with basketball shoes

1. Choose a model made for indoor courts, with a soft, grippy outsole.
2. Avoid the heaviest, bulkiest models.
3. Keep them for indoor use only.
4. Do not rely on a high collar to prevent sprains. If your ankles are a concern, talk to a doctor or physical therapist about braces.

## Shoes you should not wear

Running shoes are the one clear "no". Their soft, tall heels and forward-rolling shape are unstable when you move sideways.

## Our take

If you already own indoor basketball shoes that fit well, use them while you find out how much you like volleyball. If you are buying new shoes mainly for volleyball, a volleyball shoe is usually the better and often cheaper choice.
